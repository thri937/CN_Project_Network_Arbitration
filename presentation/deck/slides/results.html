Same lunch-hour load, 40 s measured per phase. Honest note: the cut slices pay. The canteen loses 90% of what it asks for, and this run is on one laptop (loopback), not yet over Wi-Fi.

This is the proof. We ran the lunch-hour load twice: first with automatic arbitration off, then on, measuring 20 windows of two seconds each time. With arbitration off, the shared link is overloaded and its queue is full, so every slice waits about half a second, even the clinic, which is using only 34 of its 60 kbps, and the clinic loses 5.6 percent of its data. With arbitration on, the engine cuts the cheap slices, the shared queue drains, and the clinic’s latency falls from 503 to 23 milliseconds with zero loss. The factory and the lab recover the same way. Be honest about two things. The slices that were cut pay for it: the canteen asks for ten times its 28 kbps minimum, so 90 percent of its traffic is dropped. And this measurement runs on one laptop over loopback; the phone run over the hotspot is the next measurement.
