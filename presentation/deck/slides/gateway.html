This is the heart of the testbed, and the big difference from a simulation. When a phone asks for a download at some rate, that rate is the slice’s demand, and the bytes really flow. Every 20 milliseconds the gateway runs three steps. The bytes go into the slice’s own queue, which holds half a second of traffic; if it is full, bytes are dropped. A token bucket lets them out no faster than the slice’s limit. Then all slices share one link of 950 kbps. With the default setting that is one shared queue, like a plain switch port; we also built fair sharing, deficit round robin, as an option. Every two seconds the gateway records each slice’s demand, carried rate, drops, queueing delay, latency, jitter, loss and availability. The latency comes from small probes that wait in the same queues as the data, so it is what the slice’s traffic really experiences. We measured the shaper: within a quarter of a percent of its limit at every speed we tested.
