Our next steps make it bigger, faster and more real, in four stages. Stage one: run the arbitration off-and-on test with seven real phones over the hotspot and publish those numbers. Stage two: move the shaping into the Linux kernel with traffic control, at real speeds, and add active queue management such as CoDel so the shared queue drains in milliseconds instead of seconds. Stage three: a real 5G core, Open5GS with UERANSIM simulating the phones and radio, with our limits applied through its policy function. Stage four: radio-level slicing through O-RAN. The controller does not need to change much for any of these: it only needs the same two things, read the measurements and set the limits. We also want to estimate demand from capped traffic, shape upload and phones’ own apps, and run more than one controller safely.
