Detecting contention — calmly

Reading 1

98%

Under the line. Fine.

104%

Over — wait for one more.

106%

Contention confirmed.

Demand ratio, not utilisation

Utilisation caps at 100%. Demand ÷ capacity shows how bad it is — 102% or 300%.

Hysteresis

Open above 102% for 2 readings; close after 3 calm ones. No flapping.

Cooldown

20 s between decisions, so a cut takes effect before it is judged.

Detecting contention sounds easy — if demand is above capacity, act — but that fires dozens of times a minute because demand jitters around the line. Three mechanisms from control engineering fix it. First, it measures the demand ratio, demand divided by capacity, not utilisation: utilisation can never exceed 100 percent, so it can’t tell 102 percent from 300 percent, or how much bandwidth to free. Second, hysteresis: it needs demand above 102 percent for two readings in a row to open an event, and three calm readings to close it — slow to declare victory, so it doesn’t flap. Third, a 20-second cooldown between decisions, so an applied cut has time to take effect before the loop judges it. In control-theory terms: the setpoint is capacity minus headroom, the actuator is bandwidth allocation, and the 102 percent band is the dead band.
