CBHA: Convex Business-Harm Arbitration

THE OPTIMISATION PROBLEM

minimise   Σ H_i(d_i) ← total business harm

subject to Σ freed_i ≥ Δ ← free the shortage

           0 ≤ d_i ≤ max_i ← department ceiling

           limit_i ≥ floor_i ← SLA floor

           d_i = 0 if life-safety ← compliance

Δ = Σ min(demand, limit) − 950 × (1 − 3%)

HARM OF CUTTING SLICE i BY A FRACTION d

H_i(d) = BPS_i · (d / max_i)^γ · SLA-stress · fairness · urgency · intolerance

Convex harm

γ from 1.15 to 3.0: deeper cuts hurt disproportionately more.

Water-filling

Each small step goes to the slice with the lowest marginal harm.

Carried, not requested

Only traffic a slice really carries counts; a cut that frees nothing is never chosen.

Formally, choose a degradation fraction d for each slice to minimise total business harm, subject to freeing the shortage, never exceeding each department’s ceiling, never crossing a slice’s floor, and never touching life-safety slices. One change matters on a real link: a slice can never carry more than its limit, and the rest of what it asks for is dropped in its own queue anyway. So the shortage is computed from what the slices can actually carry, the minimum of demand and limit, minus the link with a 3 percent headroom. Cutting a slice that already sends less than its new limit frees nothing, so the engine never picks it. Harm is the business score times the normalised cut raised to a power gamma, between 1.15 and 3, from recovery tolerance, which makes harm convex. The solver is water-filling: repeatedly give the next small step to the slice whose marginal harm per kbps freed is lowest. For a convex problem with one budget constraint, that is optimal up to the step size. A decision takes milliseconds.
