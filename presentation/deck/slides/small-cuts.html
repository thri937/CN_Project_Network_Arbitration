Same bandwidth freed. Harm grows faster than the cut, so spread it thinly.

Orange is bandwidth taken, blue is what remains. Both plans free exactly the same amount. But harm grows faster than the size of the cut — think of it as the cut squared. One slice cut by 60 percent: 0.6 times 0.6 is 0.36. Six slices cut by 10 percent each: six times 0.01 is 0.06 — six times less harm. So the engine keeps handing the next small piece of the cut to whichever slice it hurts least right now. In the real engine the steepness of each curve comes from the department’s recovery tolerance. Mathematically this is a convex optimisation solved by water-filling; the docs call our algorithm CBHA, Convex Business-Harm Arbitration.
