Stating limits plainly is what makes the claims we do make believable.

We want to be upfront about the limits. The numbers we showed come from software agents on one laptop over loopback; they test the real gateway and control loop but not the Wi-Fi hop, and the phone run over the hotspot is the next measurement. The shaping happens in our own application, on the downloads it generates; it is not Linux kernel traffic control and not 5G radio slicing. Only download load is generated, and the optional proxy for phones’ own apps checks that a device has joined but does not shape its traffic yet. Our gateway can see exactly what each phone asks for, which a real network cannot once a slice is capped. After a cut, the shared queue drains slowly, about 18 seconds, because it only has the 3 percent headroom to drain with; a smaller buffer or active queue management would fix that. And it is small: a 1 Mbps link, one controller, and at most eight devices on a Windows hotspot.
