Networking · Steps 5–6 · Apply and restore

Applying cuts safely, then undoing them

PHASE 1

Commit the intent

Slices, decision and audit record saved together.

PHASE 2

Enforce on the gateway

One PUT per slice; live on the next 20 ms tick.

RECONCILE

The network wins

If it refuses a limit, our record is reset to match.

RECOVER

Restore on schedule

30 s to 30 min per department, or an Admin override.

Why two phases? A database can roll back; a network that accepted a limit cannot.

Applying a decision touches two systems: our data store, which can roll back, and the network, which cannot; you cannot un-send a limit the gateway has already applied. So it happens in two phases. Phase one commits the intent: the slice changes, the decision and its audit record are saved together. Phase two pushes the new limits to the gateway, one request per slice, and they take effect on the next 20 millisecond tick. If the gateway refuses one, we reconcile: the gateway’s value wins and our record is corrected, otherwise every later decision would be computed against a fiction. Recovery runs on its own schedule, restoring each slice when its department’s recovery window ends, from 30 seconds for zero-tolerance departments to 30 minutes for the canteen; an administrator can also override a decision at once, giving a reason, and that is audited too.
